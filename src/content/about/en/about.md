I'm Eliot, a software engineer focused on <span class="highlight">AI agents and developer tooling</span>. I like long bike rides, short feedback loops, and knowing why things work.

Most recently I spent six months at <a href="https://botpress.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Botpress</a> in Montréal, where I got to both build with the product and <span class="highlight">build the product</span>. I shipped internal AI agents that run in production, built debugging tools for the agent SDK, contributed to the open-source LLM engine, and helped launch <a href="https://botpress.com/features/viber" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Viber</a>, Botpress's new agent builder you drive in plain English. Best part: shipping something and watching people use it an hour later.

I first came to Montréal for an exchange semester at Polytechnique, during my master's-level engineering degree at Grenoble INP – ESISAR. I stayed for Botpress, and I'd like to make it home. French is my first language and English my working one. I'm also eligible for a Young Professionals work permit, so <span class="highlight">hiring me doesn't need an LMIA</span>.

Before that, I worked on payments at Upflow, a YC-backed fintech in Paris, and replaced Koesio's spreadsheets with a printer configurator and margin-calculation platform: different stacks, different cities, enough variety to feel at home in the unfamiliar.

I care about <span class="highlight">doing things right</span>: not perfectly, just properly. I can't pretend something is fine when I know it isn't, which makes me a bad salesperson and, depending on who you ask, a good teammate. And when I want to understand something, I build a small, worse version of it: an HTTP server from scratch in C, a text editor in Rust, my own <a href="/projects/commits-fine-tuning">fine-tuned LLM</a>.

In October 2026 I rode <span class="highlight">2,700 km solo</span> from Grenoble to Sevilla: 18,500 m of climbing, 136 hours in the saddle, a hammock and a tarp for a home, and a hostel every time the rain won. Main takeaways: buy a lighter hammock and a bigger power bank.
